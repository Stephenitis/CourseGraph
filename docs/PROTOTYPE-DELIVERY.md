# CourseGraph prototype delivery

Revive the original CourseGraph idea as a joyful, modern academic path explorer inspired by game skill trees. Preserve the existing Rails source; the new runnable app lives in `web/`.

## Delivery scope

- A real MIT catalog subset with source links, catalog date, explicit limits, and extensible requirement expressions.
- Several named saved paths in an explicitly collapsible sidebar, with a persistent icon rail, smooth reduced-motion-aware transition, duplication, editing, Undo, shared completed-course history, and browser persistence. Export, Import, and Share stay at the bottom of the rail. No account is required. Supabase authentication and cross-device sync are not connected.
- Share is a URL snapshot of the path name, program, catalog year, selected course IDs, and terms. A recipient previews it before **Add to my saved paths** creates a new local plan without overwriting existing ones. Incoming choices become planned, retaining only the recipient's own completed history. No sender earned-credit status, advisor-review record, profile, remaining-unit estimate, or pace is transferred. There is no backend or live collaboration; invalid/catalog-mismatched links show readable errors and large paths use file export.
- An unchosen graph card's click, tap, or keyboard Enter/Space adds it to the active path in the selected semester and opens the compact below-map preview. Clicking a planned card or its checked circle again unselects it. If supported planned dependents would also lose valid requirements, show their names and terms before confirmation; Cancel leaves the path untouched and one Undo restores the whole removal. Respect valid OR/count/unit alternatives and chronological prerequisite/corequisite rules. Completed history stays untouched; other already-missing/manual-review courses are not swept into the removal. Course-list entries, detail links, timeline cards stay inspect-only. All choices persist locally in the current browser; saving is not enrollment or degree certification.
- The fixed **Stephen’s** heading and generated student placeholder portrait are local prototype profile placeholders, with no authentication or account action. They must not imply a signed-in or institution-verified identity.
- Autofill has been removed from the interface; paths are assembled through individual course choices.
- Hover/focus courses to animate incoming prerequisite edges in cyan and outgoing enabling edges in violet. Missing prerequisite nodes are amber, missing edges are red with text warnings. Preserve AND/OR alternatives and avoid claiming all downstream courses are unlocked.
- Semester planning and conflicts when actual section meeting times exist. Unknown future schedules must stay unknown.
- Source-linked MIT workload guidance, eight-term course loads, optional advisor-confirmed remaining-unit capacity scenarios, and a clear distinction from a graduation forecast.
- Start in Full map with a left-to-right graph from foundations toward a program goal on the right. Use a distinct textured cool-slate canvas and frame, with a compact program row and view navigation in the sidebar. Remove the Search control and Program goal shortcut while retaining the actual crest on the map. Aim for a wider, shallow rectangle near 1.95:1 overall, with deterministic organic spacing and calm obstacle-aware curves. Selecting a course opens its compact preview below the map at every width, preserving map width and readable course zoom. Preserve full course titles, breathing room, and a small navigable overview. Include all available program subjects and recursive prerequisites/corequisites; the catalog remains partial and hover preserves placement; selecting a course gently expands nearby spacing without reordering branches.
- Source-backed **Foundation, Core, Advanced, and Graduate** curriculum roles use white, blue, purple, and charcoal surfaces, with a neutral **Undergraduate** fallback. Roles may vary by program, using documented MIT groups and published levels; real prerequisite ordering takes precedence. They are not difficulty scores or class years. Cards omit four-bar ratings and Inferred/Provisional tags. A compact **Course numbers & labels** arrow-marked helper beside the legend in the light-grey guide row explains the labels and MIT numbering; [CURRICULUM-ROLES.md](CURRICULUM-ROLES.md) records the sources. Cards use a fixed 336×252, 4:3 footprint and full-width bold titles without subject icons. Chosen courses retain their curriculum color with a pastel-green check; focus adds an outline. A focused locked course becomes yellow with a prominent lock. Semantic connection colors remain consistent.
- A large original enamel program-goal crest in cardinal, ivory, and bronze, combining a beacon star, circuits, open book, and laurel within a layered metallic silhouette. Its 540×550 artwork area is intentionally prominent in the overview, with the full program label and mapped-coursework progress bar underneath. Completed coverage is the earned fill; planned coverage includes completed coursework and must not be added to it. Percentages cover only modeled requirements. A milestone check and subtle global appearance change follow explicit student confirmation of an advisor review and major fulfillment, independently of the percentage. This is user-recorded, not institutionally verified. Further study remains subject to separate advising/admission; no doctoral curriculum is ingested.
- Review records are scoped to the path, major, catalog year, and completed-course membership. Clear them after relevant changes or when copying/importing into a new path identity; preserve ordinary planned-course edits. Allow removal and Undo. No automatic fulfillment from the mapped subset or zero remaining units, and no implied degree award or GIR completion.
- An **Explore the field** panel with relevant MIT talks and short lectures. Preserve official source links and distinguish editorial program connections from university recommendations.
- MIT is the default interface theme, with Plain, Dark, NYU, Stanford, and Virginia Tech alternatives. Use simple university accents, a clear MIT program-goal identity, wrapping sidebar labels, and distinct supporting-panel surfaces. All themes use the same MIT data and retain graph semantics and curriculum-role treatments. This is an independent prototype with partial catalog coverage, not an endorsed university product.
- Accessible keyboard interactions, reduced-motion support, responsive layout, clear uncertainty, no admissions guarantees or unsupported degree-audit claims.
- Public deployment at [coursegraph.my](https://coursegraph.my), with [the platform address](https://coursegraph-paths.rlze.chatgpt.site) also available. Active routing, TLS, and a successful HTTPS response are recorded in [deployment verification](DEPLOYMENT.md).
